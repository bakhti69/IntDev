Who built what.

Problem and approach

One camera, one angle, no labels. Instead of training a video classifier on footage we do not have, we turn the video into trajectories on a map of this junction and express every class as a rule over them — the same definitions the annotators use (start/end conventions included).

What is learned, what is rule-based

learned Road-user detection only: YOLO11s pre-trained on COCO (Ultralytics weights, no fine-tuning — we have no labels for this camera).

rule-based Everything after detection: tracking, the scene map, signal reading, all 14 event classes and the Part B risk score. Rules are deterministic, explainable and unit-tested on synthetic trajectories drawn on the real map.

Why: the hidden test set contains events that are not in the samples, so any classifier trained on the samples would not have seen them. Rules written from the class definitions do not need examples.

Key design decisions

  • Speeds in body lengths per second (image speed / √(box area)): removes most of the perspective, so one threshold works near and far.
  • Scene registration. The framing moves between clips (up to 60 px and ~1° in our stills), so the map is re-registered on every video with SIFT + RANSAC.
  • Signal heads that face away. The queue approaching the camera has its signal facing away; its red phase is inferred from the pedestrian WALK head and from vehicles held at the stop line.
  • Stopped vs queued: a stopped vehicle is one that other traffic keeps passing.

Technical report

What we built

A detector → tracker → map → rules pipeline (Part A), a causal time-to-collision risk model (Part B), and the tooling around it: scene registration, a labeling page for dev sets, annotated renders, EDA, a Gradio demo and 22 unit tests with synthetic trajectories on the real layout.

What worked

  • SIFT registration fits the layout on every still, including dusk frames shifted by 60 px.
  • COCO YOLO11s finds cars, buses, pedestrians and cyclists reliably in day and dusk frames.
  • Reading the pedestrian head fixed the "signal faces away" problem for the queue approach.
  • Body-length-normalised speed made one set of thresholds work across the depth of the scene.

What did not (or is not proven)

  • Rules were tuned on synthetic trajectories and public clips, not on labelled sample video yet — the numbers below are what we can honestly claim.
  • Top-down footage breaks COCO detection (cars read as "cell phones").
  • Pixel cues (obstacle, fire) needed camera-drift compensation and shape filters to stop firing on lane markings; smoke without flames is not detected.
  • illegal_turn needs the junction's prohibited movements; until confirmed it is never predicted, because a predicted class that never occurs costs a whole class in the macro-F1.
  • Near-miss vs normal hard braking in a queue is the least certain boundary.

Next

  • Label the samples with the labeling page and tune thresholds per class at IoU 0.7.
  • Fine-tune the detector on frames of this camera (dusk, occlusion in the queue).
  • Learned re-scoring of accident / near-miss candidates on public crash datasets (CCD, DoTA).
  • Calibrate the risk score on those datasets so 0.5 means "probably within 5 s".
